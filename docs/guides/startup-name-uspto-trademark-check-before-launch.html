How to Screen USPTO Class 9 & Class 42 Trademarks Before Buying a Startup Domain

An available .ai or .io domain offers zero legal protection if a live federal USPTO registration exists in Nice Class 009 (downloadable apps/software) or Class 042 (SaaS/cloud platforms).

1. The 5 Nice Classes Every Software Founder Must Check

Trademark collisions depend on likelihood of confusion within overlapping goods and services. For SaaS, AI apps, and developer tools, always screen IC 009 (downloadable software/mobile apps), IC 035 (advertising/marketplaces), IC 038 (messaging/telecom), IC 041 (publishing/education), and IC 042 (SaaS/cloud computing).

2. Screen Sound-Alikes (Phonetic Collisions), Not Just Exact Spellings

The USPTO refuses marks that sound identical even with trendy startup spelling swaps (e.g., KwikPay vs. QuickPay, or Lyft vs. Lift). Automated phonetic + edit-distance screening catches these early before you spend $350+ on non-refundable filing fees.

3. Combine USPTO Screening with App Store & Package Checks

Even unregistered common-law marks on the Apple App Store, Google Play, npm, or GitHub can create immediate customer confusion and App Store rejection. Screen all four layers together, then have a trademark attorney run a formal clearance search on your top 1–2 finalists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a DEAD or CANCELLED USPTO trademark block my startup name?

A dead federal registration is no longer federally active, though the former owner might still hold common-law rights if they are actively selling under the name. Always inspect live marks first.

Is automated USPTO screening legal advice?

No. Automated screening eliminates obvious conflicts in seconds so you only pay a trademark attorney to review clean finalists.

Fixing SPF PermError (Too Many DNS Lookups > 10) for SaaS Domains

RFC 7208 caps SPF evaluation at 10 DNS-querying mechanisms including nested includes. Crossing 11 lookups causes immediate SPF PermError across Gmail and Microsoft 365.

1. Why Common SaaS Stacks Blow Past 10 Lookups Fast

A single include: statement often hides 3 to 5 nested DNS queries. Combining Google Workspace (3–4), HubSpot (2–3), Salesforce/Zendesk (3+), and Mailchimp easily hits 12–16 lookups.

# Typical broken apex SPF (13+ recursive lookups -> PermError!):
"v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:spf.protection.outlook.com include:sendgrid.net include:mail.zendesk.com include:servers.mcsv.net ~all"

2. Fix #1 — Move Third-Party Senders to Subdomains

Send transactional mail from mail.yourdomain.com and marketing campaigns from news.yourdomain.com. Each subdomain gets its own fresh 10-lookup SPF budget and isolates sender reputation.

3. Fix #2 — Remove Dead Includes & Flatten Static Ranges

Strip legacy tools you no longer pay for, remove redundant a/mx mechanisms, and use an automated SPF flattener (included in InboxShield Pro's flatten compiler) with a scheduled GitHub Action to catch provider IP rotations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do ip4: and ip6: directives count toward the 10 SPF DNS lookup limit?

No. Only DNS-querying mechanisms (include, a, mx, ptr, exists, redirect) count toward the 10-lookup ceiling.

Can I publish two separate v=spf1 TXT records to get 20 lookups?

Never. Publishing two v=spf1 TXT records on the same host causes an instant RFC 7208 PermError on every mail server.
